# HASTABAZAAR SELLER AGREEMENT — CHANGE AND VERIFICATION MEMO

**Related document:** `Hastabazaar-Seller-Marketplace-Agreement-Draft-v2.md`  
**Status:** Draft for legal and commercial verification

This memo explains what changed, why it changed, and what should be reverified before the Agreement is finalized. It is not a substitute for advice from a licensed Nepal lawyer or tax adviser.

## 1. Legal framework corrected

### What changed

The Agreement now defines Applicable Law by reference to the current Electronic Commerce (E-Commerce) Act, 2081, Electronic Transactions Act, Consumer Protection Act, Individual Privacy Act, tax/payment law, and subordinate directives. Informal research notes and corrupted citation artifacts were removed from the contract text.

### Why

The earlier draft referred generally to Nepal’s e-commerce law but contained research annotations such as “NepalLaws,” “Law Commission Repository,” “+1,” and invalid characters. Those should not become contractual wording.

### Benefit

The contract stays readable and continues to apply if a statute is amended or replaced.

### Reverify

- Official Electronic Commerce Act: https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/13517/electricity-trade--e-workers--act--2081/
- Official Electronic Commerce Directive, 2082: https://doc.gov.np/content/338/electronic-business--e-commerce--directory--2082/
- Electronic Transactions Act: https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/13397/
- Consumer Protection Act official English PDF: https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/app/public/275/posts/1721033312_37.pdf

Counsel should confirm the Agreement against the controlling Nepali text and any amendments or notices effective on the final execution date.

## 2. Exact Party identity and Seller evidence added

### What changed

The preamble now requires Hastabazaar’s exact registered legal name. Schedule 1 captures each Seller’s legal form, registration, PAN/VAT, addresses, representative, licences, settlement account, and accepted version.

### Why

Referring only to whatever happens to be in a changeable Seller Account is weaker evidence of the contracting party.

### Benefit

An immutable acceptance snapshot improves KYC, invoicing, enforcement, and proof of electronic consent.

### Reverify

Insert the exact entity information shown on Hastabazaar’s registration, PAN/VAT, and e-commerce listing certificates.

## 3. Electronic acceptance strengthened

### What changed

Acceptance now requires an affirmative unticked checkbox or other valid method, authority confirmation, access to downloadable terms, version records, and renewed acceptance for material changes.

### Why

“Continuing to sell” is less reliable as the only proof of agreement to major financial or liability changes.

### Benefit

This produces clearer evidence of notice and consent while supporting scalable online onboarding.

### Reverify

Confirm that the actual Seller Centre can store the listed records and present downloadable policy versions.

## 4. Marketplace role clarified without disclaiming statutory duties

### What changed

The Seller remains the supplier, but Clause 2.6 expressly preserves Hastabazaar’s non-waivable intermediary and grievance obligations. The broad “marketplace agent” label was replaced with specific limited authority.

### Why

Marketplace status does not automatically exempt the Platform from duties to Customers. Broad agency language could also create unintended authority.

### Benefit

The clause preserves the intended marketplace model without suggesting that the Customer must chase the Seller or carrier whenever the Platform has a legal duty.

### Reverify

Counsel should verify the allocation against Sections 14, 18, 20, and 33 of the E-Commerce Act.

## 5. Individual-maker eligibility narrowed

### What changed

An individual need not form a private company, but must use a legally eligible and registered form and hold applicable tax registration before selling.

### Why

The earlier language could be read as allowing informal commercial activation.

### Benefit

Hastabazaar can support artisans without weakening registration and tax compliance.

### Reverify

Obtain Nepal counsel or Department confirmation on the exact registrations required for each maker category and municipality/province.

## 6. Statutory listing fields completed

### What changed

Clause 8 adds final tax-inclusive price, delivery charges, payment method, actual producer, imported origin, cancellation before dispatch, post-sale terms, main contract terms, and review/grievance mechanisms.

### Why

These details align the Seller’s information duty with Section 6 of the E-Commerce Act.

### Benefit

Complete listings reduce disputes, misleading-advertising risk, and return rates.

### Reverify

Check whether each field exists in the Seller listing form and Customer product page; contract wording alone is insufficient.

## 7. Handmade claims made auditable and fair

### What changed

The Agreement distinguishes fully handmade, tool-assisted, assembled, designed-but-outsourced, customized, commercial, and sourced Products. It adds evidence, confidentiality, decision notice, and response rights.

### Why

“Handmade” can otherwise be used inconsistently, while an unrestricted inspection power may expose genuine trade secrets.

### Benefit

Customers receive honest origin information and legitimate makers receive a predictable verification process.

### Reverify

Finalize objective badge and handmade-percentage/process criteria in a separate policy.

## 8. Order formation defined

### What changed

An Order is accepted when confirmation is issued following Seller or enabled automated acceptance. Payment authorization alone is not necessarily acceptance.

### Why

The original draft did not identify when the binding Seller–Customer sale is formed.

### Benefit

This reduces disputes over inventory errors, pricing errors, cancellation, and processing deadlines.

### Reverify

Ensure the clause matches the real checkout, payment, auto-acceptance, and notification flow.

## 9. Automatic 20% delay penalty retained and standardized

### What changed

The exact commercial rule requested by Hastabazaar is retained:

> Hastabazaar does impose an automatic twenty percent (20%) delayed-fulfilment penalty on a Delayed Order.

The Agreement now defines:

- the 20% calculation base;
- partial-Order treatment;
- why the penalty exists;
- automatic initial assessment;
- notice and evidence;
- a three-Business-Day Seller response;
- review and reasoned decision;
- exclusions;
- settlement deduction;
- correction of errors;
- non-compounding cap; and
- prevention of duplicate recovery.

### Why

The previous sentence did not identify 20% of what, when delay occurs, who is responsible, or how a Seller can demonstrate carrier or Platform fault.

### Benefit

The new procedure makes the rule consistent, calculable, auditable, and less likely to punish Sellers for events outside their control. It also explains the operational and Customer-service impact the percentage is intended to address.

### Legal caution to reverify

The National Civil Code provisions on breach compensation indicate that where a contract predetermines an amount, recoverable compensation may be limited to a reasonable amount not exceeding the stated amount. Calling a deduction “automatic” does not guarantee that a court will enforce 20% in every factual situation. Nepal counsel should specifically approve:

1. use of the word “penalty” rather than “agreed service-level charge” or “pre-estimated compensation”;
2. the 20% rate and calculation base;
3. evidence supporting likely operational loss;
4. the response and review periods; and
5. whether VAT should be removed from the base in every category.

Official Civil Code source: https://lawcommission.gov.np/content/13455/civil-code-2074/

## 10. “Similarly” retained in formal language

### What changed

The wording is now:

> “Similarly, and without limiting the automatic delayed-fulfilment penalty, a Seller-caused delay may, having regard to its seriousness, frequency, Customer impact, and the Seller’s response, result in one or more of the following proportionate measures…”

### Why

The earlier “Simlarily” was misspelled and did not explain whether the following measures were additional, alternative, or automatic.

### Benefit

The revised text keeps the requested transition while making additional sanctions proportionate and reviewable.

### Reverify

Finalize the thresholds for warnings, visibility reduction, order limitation, suspension, and termination in the Seller Performance Policy.

## 11. Logistics and Customer remedy separated

### What changed

The Customer-facing remedy is handled independently of the later allocation of cost among Hastabazaar, Seller, and carrier.

### Why

The prior “facilitate a carrier claim” language could imply that a Customer or Seller must wait for the carrier dispute.

### Benefit

Customer resolution can occur on time, while the responsible party is charged later based on packaging, custody, handover evidence, and fault.

### Reverify

Align carrier contracts, insurance, declared-value limits, and scan/handover procedures with Clause 15.

## 12. Returns and refunds made operational

### What changed

The Agreement preserves mandatory rights, rejects blanket no-return terms, allocates Seller/Platform/carrier fault, addresses customized goods, prevents conditional refunds, and sets a seven-day processing target when a refund is approved or required.

### Why

The Consumer Protection Act contains a general seven-day return framework and a fifteen-day rule for qualifying sealed goods with an unbroken seal, subject to statutory exceptions. The E-Commerce Act and current Directive add electronic-commerce requirements.

### Benefit

Customer rights are protected while internal financial responsibility remains traceable.

### Reverify

Counsel must reconcile the Platform Return Policy with the Consumer Protection Act, E-Commerce Act, Directive 2082, and product-specific laws. Confirm the correct trigger for the seven-day refund period.

## 13. Grievance process added

### What changed

Hastabazaar must register and acknowledge complaints, investigate them, and ordinarily issue a reasoned decision within fifteen days. The Seller retains a forty-eight-hour internal response duty.

### Why

The Seller response deadline alone did not satisfy the Platform’s own grievance-management obligations.

### Benefit

The Platform can meet its Customer deadline even if a Seller is unresponsive.

### Reverify

Build or verify the online complaint log, acknowledgement message, case owner, evidence workflow, decision template, and escalation process.

## 14. Fake reviews and rating manipulation prohibited

### What changed

The Agreement expressly prohibits self-review, related-person reviews, purchased reviews, fabricated feedback, review pressure, and suppression of legitimate complaints.

### Why

The E-Commerce Act specifically addresses pseudo-consumer reviews and unfair conduct.

### Benefit

The rule protects marketplace trust and provides a clear contractual basis for moderation.

## 15. Payment, invoice, tax, and settlement structure added

### What changed

Clauses 20–22 allocate Customer invoices, Platform fee invoices, approved payment channels, settlement statements, deductions, reserves, reconciliation, tax withholding, and five-year record retention.

### Why

The submitted draft ended mid-fee clause and contained no complete settlement mechanism.

### Benefit

Sellers can understand what they earn, what can be deducted, and how to challenge an error.

### Reverify before use

Complete every placeholder in Schedule 2, especially:

- commission;
- settlement trigger and cycle;
- RTO and reverse-logistics allocation;
- reserve percentage and duration;
- minimum payout;
- payment-processing fees; and
- VAT/TDS invoicing workflow.

Obtain Nepal tax advice on marketplace invoicing and withholding.

## 16. Data protection and security added

### What changed

Customer data is restricted to fulfilment, warranty, complaints, fraud prevention, and legal compliance. Sale of data, unrelated marketing, unauthorized disclosure, and off-platform bypass are prohibited. Security and incident-notification duties were added.

### Why

The original draft did not govern Seller access to Customer addresses, phone numbers, or Order history.

### Benefit

The clause reduces misuse, leakage, spam, and regulatory risk.

### Reverify

Confirm encryption, access control, retention, deletion, breach response, and vendor-processing practices against Directive 2082 and the Individual Privacy Act.

## 17. Intellectual property provisions added

### What changed

The Seller retains its content but grants a limited operational and marketing licence. Third-party infringement, takedown, archival retention, Platform ownership, and de-identified analytics are addressed.

### Why

Hastabazaar otherwise lacks clear permission to resize photographs, promote listings, translate descriptions, or retain evidence after termination.

### Benefit

The Platform receives the rights it needs without taking ownership of the maker’s work.

### Reverify

Confirm the desired territory, translation rights, advertising channels, and post-termination promotional wind-down.

## 18. Suspension procedure made proportionate

### What changed

Ordinary issues receive notice and an opportunity to cure; urgent fraud, safety, illegality, counterfeit, data, or regulatory cases allow immediate action. An appeal and payout protection are included.

### Why

Broad unrestricted suspension powers can be commercially unfair and difficult to administer consistently.

### Benefit

The Platform can act quickly in emergencies while providing procedural fairness in ordinary cases.

### Reverify

Approve performance thresholds, response periods, appeal ownership, and emergency categories.

## 19. Indemnity and liability added

### What changed

The Seller indemnifies Hastabazaar for product, infringement, authenticity, tax, packaging, data, and fraud risks attributable to the Seller. Hastabazaar and carrier fault are excluded. Claims procedure, direct-loss treatment, mandatory-law carve-outs, and a placeholder Platform liability cap are included.

### Why

The submitted portion had remedies but no complete third-party claim or liability framework.

### Benefit

Liability follows control and fault, with notice and defence protections.

### Reverify

The liability cap in Clause 28.5 is deliberately unfinished. The board and Nepal counsel must approve the amount and carve-outs.

## 20. Policy amendments, termination, and disputes added

### What changed

Material fee, penalty, settlement, and liability changes generally require advance notice and renewed acceptance. Termination, pending Orders, survival, governing law, internal resolution, statutory remedies, and court jurisdiction are included.

### Why

These provisions were absent from the submitted text.

### Benefit

The Parties know how changes take effect, how the relationship ends, and where disputes are addressed.

### Reverify

Approve:

- material-change notice period;
- Seller and Platform termination periods;
- cure period;
- whether disputes use Nepal courts, mediation, or arbitration; and
- the agreed district/forum.
